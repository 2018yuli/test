## 狗狗的故事：一只叫“团子”的小狗

## A Dog’s Story: A Little Dog Named “Tuanzi”

镇子不大，路口有一家面包店。每天清晨，面包出炉的香气会先飘到门口那棵梧桐树下——那里总趴着一只白色的小狗，耳尖带点浅黄，像刚蘸过一点蜂蜜。大家都叫它“团子”，因为它蜷起来的时候圆滚滚的，像一团温热的云。

The town wasn’t big, and there was a bakery at the intersection. Every morning, the scent of fresh bread would drift first to the plane tree by the door—beneath it always lay a small white dog, the tips of its ears tinted a pale yellow, as if dipped in a touch of honey. Everyone called it “Tuanzi,” because when it curled up it became round and fluffy, like a warm little cloud.

团子不属于任何一家人，却又像属于整条街。面包店的阿姨会把卖剩的边角装进小纸袋，轻轻放在梧桐树旁；修自行车的叔叔在团子路过时，总会把扳手举高一点，怕吓着它；放学的小孩会把书包往上一提，蹲下来摸摸它的脑袋，悄悄告诉它今天数学又没考好。

Tuanzi didn’t belong to any one family, yet it somehow belonged to the whole street. The bakery auntie would pack leftover crusts into a small paper bag and set it gently by the plane tree; the man who fixed bicycles would always lift his wrench a little higher when Tuanzi passed, afraid of startling it; children on their way home from school would hike their backpacks up, squat down to pat its head, and quietly confess that they hadn’t done well on their math test again.

团子最拿手的事，是“送人回家”。傍晚时分，街灯还没完全亮起，团子就会从面包店门口站起来，抖抖毛，开始沿着街道慢慢巡逻。谁要是走得匆忙、情绪低落，团子就会悄悄跟在后面，保持半步的距离——不打扰，但也不离开。要是对方回头，团子就装作在闻地上的落叶；要是对方停下，团子就坐下，像在说：“我在。”

What Tuanzi was best at was “walking people home.” At dusk, before the streetlights were fully on, it would rise from its spot by the bakery, shake out its fur, and begin a slow patrol along the street. If someone walked in a hurry or looked downhearted, Tuanzi would quietly follow behind, keeping a half-step of distance—never intruding, never leaving. If they turned around, Tuanzi would pretend to sniff the fallen leaves; if they stopped, Tuanzi would sit, as if to say, “I’m here.”

有一天，镇上来了场突如其来的暴雨。雨点砸在伞面上噼啪作响，路边的排水口被落叶堵住，水漫过了鞋面。面包店的阿姨因为着急回家，匆匆锁门时把钥匙掉在了台阶缝里。她找了半天，越找越急，雨水顺着脸颊往下流——也不知道是雨还是眼泪。

One day, a sudden downpour swept through town. Raindrops drummed and cracked against umbrellas; fallen leaves clogged the street drains, and water rose over the tops of shoes. The bakery auntie, rushing to get home, dropped her key into a crack between the steps as she hurriedly locked up. She searched for a long time, growing more frantic by the minute, rain streaming down her cheeks—who could tell whether it was rain or tears.

这时，团子从雨里跑过来，浑身湿透，像一团被打湿的棉花。它没有叫，只是绕着台阶转了两圈，鼻子贴着地面闻了又闻，最后停在一处最不起眼的缝隙前，前爪轻轻一刨——金属钥匙“叮”的一声弹了出来。阿姨愣了两秒，笑着把钥匙捡起，蹲下来把团子抱进怀里。那天晚上，面包店门口多了一个小小的塑料盒，里面铺着干净的旧毛巾，还有一张写着歪歪扭扭字迹的纸条：

Just then, Tuanzi came running through the rain, soaked through like a lump of wet cotton. It didn’t bark. It simply circled the steps twice, nose to the ground, sniffing again and again, until it stopped at the most unremarkable crack. With a gentle scrape of its front paw—the metal key sprang out with a clear “ding.” The auntie froze for a couple of seconds, then smiled as she picked up the key, crouched down, and pulled Tuanzi into her arms. That night, a small plastic box appeared outside the bakery, lined with a clean old towel, along with a note written in wobbly handwriting:

“团子，今天谢谢你。以后下雨也有地方躲。”

“Tuanzi, thank you for today. From now on, you’ll have a place to hide when it rains.”

从那以后，团子依旧不属于哪一家，但它有了一个固定的“家门口”。清晨，它在梧桐树下等第一缕面包香；傍晚，它在街灯亮起前送人回家；下雨天，它会钻进那个小盒子里，把尾巴盖在鼻尖上，睡得很安稳。

After that, Tuanzi still didn’t belong to any one household, but it had a steady “doorstep” of its own. In the morning, it waited beneath the plane tree for the first hint of bread in the air; at dusk, it walked people home before the streetlights came on; on rainy days, it would curl up in that little box, tail covering the tip of its nose, and sleep in perfect peace.

有人说，狗狗的厉害不在于听懂多少话，而在于记得住谁需要陪伴。团子听不懂“谢谢”，却懂得走在你身边；它不会写字，却把温柔写进每一天的脚印里。

Some say a dog’s gift isn’t how many words it understands, but how well it remembers who needs company. Tuanzi may not understand “thank you,” but it understands walking by your side; it can’t write, but it writes tenderness into the footprints it leaves each day.
